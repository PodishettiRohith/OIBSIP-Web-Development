Alan Turing

The Father of Modern Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence

Biography

Alan Mathison Turing (23 June 1912 – 7 June 1954) was an English mathematician, computer scientist, logician, cryptanalyst, philosopher, and theoretical biologist. He was highly influential in the development of theoretical computer science, providing a formalisation of the concepts of algorithm and computation with the Turing machine.

During the Second World War, Turing was a leading participant in war-code breaking at Bletchley Park. He played a pivotal role in cracking intercepted coded messages, including the German Enigma machine, enabling the Allies to defeat the Axis powers in many crucial engagements.

After the war, Turing designed the Automatic Computing Engine (ACE), which was one of the first designs for a stored-program computer. In 1950, he proposed an experiment to test machine intelligence, which came to be known as the "Turing Test," a concept that remains fundamental in artificial intelligence discussions today.

"Sometimes it is the people no one imagines anything of who do the things that no one can imagine."

— Alan Turing

Key Achievements & Timeline

  1. 1936: Published paper introducing the theoretical concept of the "Universal Turing Machine."
  2. 1939-1945: Led the Hut 8 section at Bletchley Park, building the Bombe machine to crack Enigma codes.
  3. 1946: Designed the Automatic Computing Engine (ACE), a precursor to the modern computer.
  4. 1950: Published "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" introducing the famous Turing Test.
  5. 2013: Posthumously granted a royal pardon by Queen Elizabeth II, acknowledging his monumental contributions.
